04 · Why?

Why did such a small change matter?

One infection isn't an outcome, it's an opportunity. Every day someone is infectious they meet people, and each meeting is a chance to make another infection — which then gets its own days, and its own meetings. The question is never how bad one case is. It's whether each case, on average, makes more than one more.

A toy model, not a forecast: 180 people, an eight-day illness, and one seeded random number generator. Same seed every run, so the only thing that ever changes is you.

The change you made wasn't added to the outbreak. It was multiplied through it.

Nobody in this town could feel the difference — five contacts a day and six look the same from the inside.

Which is the whole problem: the one number that decides this is the one number nobody experiences.
