One person is infected. Everyone else is healthy. Press start and watch what ten contacts a day does.

The point

Nothing about the virus moved. What moved was a number that gets multiplied, not added: how many people you meet, times the chance a meeting passes it on, times how long you stay infectious. That product is how many people the average infected person hands it to.

Above one, every generation is bigger than the last and the outbreak finds almost everyone. Below one, every generation is smaller and it runs out of people before it runs out of luck. In this town the line sits between five and six contacts a day. One meeting either side of it is the difference between ten people and a hundred and forty — which is why the honest advice was never "stop seeing anyone", and never "carry on". It was: you are closer to the line than it feels.

A toy model, not a forecast: 180 people, an eight-day illness, and one seeded random number generator. Same seed every run, so the only thing that ever changes is you.
